GEO vs SEO: what actually changes

Comparison7 min read·Updated 2026-08-31
The short answer

SEO earns you a position on a results page. GEO earns you a mention inside an answer. Roughly 60% of the work overlaps — crawlability, clean structure, useful content — but GEO adds three things SEO never needed: passage-level extractability, explicit entity definition through structured data, and off-site brand corroboration. You need both: engines cannot cite what they cannot crawl.

The one-line difference

SEO answers the question "where do I appear in a list?" GEO answers "am I in the answer at all?" That shift sounds small and changes almost everything downstream, because a list has ten slots and an answer has about five sources — and no second page.

What carries straight over

Do not throw away your SEO work. These fundamentals matter as much or more than before:

  • Crawlability and indexation. If GPTBot, ClaudeBot or PerplexityBot cannot fetch the page, nothing else you do matters.
  • Clean, server-rendered HTML. Content locked behind heavy client-side JavaScript is a real risk — not every AI crawler executes it.
  • Site speed and information architecture. Same reasons as always.
  • Genuinely useful content. Thin content failed at SEO and fails harder at GEO, where the engine is judging whether a passage is worth quoting.

What is genuinely new

1. You optimize passages, not pages

An engine rarely cites "your homepage." It cites the two sentences that answered the question. That changes how you write: every section needs to make sense lifted out of context, with the answer first and the elaboration after. Burying the answer in paragraph six — standard practice in engagement-optimized content marketing — is actively counterproductive.

2. You define your entity explicitly

SEO could survive on ambiguity about who you are. GEO cannot. The engine needs to resolve "BAIDLABS" into a specific organization with a location, a founder, services and corroborating profiles before it will confidently recommend you. That's what structured data is for — an explicit, machine-readable statement of identity and relationships.

3. Authority moved from links to mentions

This is the biggest strategic change. Ahrefs found brand mentions correlate with AI visibility at roughly 0.66, versus 0.22 for backlinks. Being talked about now beats being linked to — which means digital PR, community presence, reviews and directory consistency matter more than a link-building campaign.

The counterintuitive part: ranking isn't required

Research on AI Overviews has repeatedly found that a large majority of cited pages sit outside the classic organic top 10. The engine is selecting for passage relevance and source credibility, not position. This is genuinely good news if you're a smaller player: you don't have to outrank an incumbent to be cited alongside them — you have to answer the specific question better.

Practical implication. If you have pages ranking on page two or three that answer real questions well, they may be closer to an AI citation than to a top-three ranking. Rewriting them answer-first is often faster ROI than chasing links.

How to run both together

Treat SEO as the foundation and GEO as the layer above it. In practice that means one workflow, not two teams:

  1. Keep the technical SEO baseline healthy — crawl access, speed, clean markup, sitemaps.
  2. Add the entity layer — Organization, Person, Service and FAQ schema that accurately reflects visible content.
  3. Restructure content answer-first, with sourced statistics and quotable passages.
  4. Measure differently — track citations and share of voice per engine, not just rankings. See how to measure AI visibility.
  5. Invest in off-site mentions rather than links alone.

Frequently asked

Should I stop doing SEO? +
No. Engines cannot cite pages they cannot crawl, and much of what feeds AI answers comes from conventional search indexes. Think of SEO as the floor and GEO as the game played on top of it.
Will GEO hurt my SEO rankings? +
No — the changes overlap heavily with SEO best practice. Answer-first structure, credible sourcing, accurate schema and clean markup are things classic search engines also reward. We have yet to see a well-executed GEO change harm rankings.
Which matters more for a new business? +
GEO, usually — because AI answers are less winner-take-all by domain authority than classic rankings. A new site with genuinely better answers and clean entity signals can get cited alongside established players far sooner than it can outrank them.
