GEO for hotels and resorts

Industry6 min read·Updated 2026-08-31
The short answer

AI trip planning has moved a large share of hotel discovery away from OTA browsing toward conversational shortlisting — 'where should I stay in Jaipur for a heritage experience with a pool, under ₹8,000?' Properties get named when their attributes are explicit and machine-readable, and when independent reviews corroborate the claim. Being listed on an OTA is not the same as being retrievable.

The shift in how a stay gets chosen

The old path: search a destination, open an OTA, filter, compare, book. The new path increasingly starts with a conversation — describing a trip and asking what fits. The engine returns a handful of named properties with reasons.

That is a shortlist formed before any booking site is opened. If you're not in it, the OTA listing you're paying commission on never gets seen.

Why attribute clarity decides everything

Travellers ask in attributes, not names: heritage character, rooftop pool, walkable to the old city, quiet for remote work, good for families with toddlers, under a specific budget. An engine can only match a property to those attributes if the attributes are stated somewhere it can read.

Most hotel sites lead with atmospheric copy — "an oasis of timeless elegance" — and bury or omit the concrete facts. The result is a beautiful site that cannot be matched to a query. The fix is unglamorous and effective: state the specifics plainly somewhere on the page.

The attribute checklist

  • ✓ Precise location and what's genuinely walkable, with distances
  • ✓ Property type and character — heritage haveli, boutique, business, resort
  • ✓ Room types with sizes, occupancy and bed configuration
  • ✓ Honest price bands by season
  • ✓ Amenities stated as facts, not adjectives — pool (heated? hours?), wifi speed, parking, restaurant timings
  • ✓ Suitability: families, couples, solo travellers, business, accessibility provisions
  • ✓ Policies: check-in times, pets, cancellation

Reviews are your strongest external signal

Hospitality is the most review-mediated category there is, and AI answers lean on that corpus heavily. Volume, recency and rating all matter — and so does the language reviewers use. If guests consistently describe your property as "quiet and great for working," that phrase becomes retrievable evidence for a query about remote-work stays.

This gives you a subtle lever: the experiences you deliver and highlight shape the vocabulary of your reviews, and that vocabulary becomes your attribute footprint.

Own the local expertise questions

Beyond your own property, publish what you genuinely know about the destination — the best time to visit, how many days to allow, what's worth skipping, how to get from the airport, where to eat nearby. These questions are asked far more often than "where should I stay," and answering them well makes your property the source in the wider trip conversation.

Structured data

Use Hotel / LodgingBusiness schema with address, geo coordinates, star rating, amenity features, check-in and check-out times, and price range. This is one of the richest schema vocabularies available and one of the most underused in the industry.

Independence matters. Every booking that comes from an AI recommendation to your own site rather than through an OTA is a booking without commission. The economics of getting this right are unusually direct in hospitality.

Frequently asked

Do OTA listings help my AI visibility? +
They contribute — OTA pages and their reviews are frequently retrieved sources. But they position the OTA as the destination, not you. Your own site needs the same attribute clarity so you can be recommended directly.
How do I compete with big hotel chains? +
By being specific where they're generic. Chains publish templated property pages; an independent property can describe its actual character, neighbourhood and best-fit guest in detail. Conversational queries are attribute-matched, which favours the property that stated its attributes clearly.
What about non-English queries? +
Increasingly relevant, and mostly unaddressed. If you serve travellers from specific regions, having genuine content in their language is a real edge — the competitive field in non-English travel queries is far emptier.
