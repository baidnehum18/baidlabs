GEO for real estate

Industry6 min read·Updated 2026-08-31
The short answer

Property buyers use AI engines for the research phase — neighbourhood comparisons, price expectations, builder reputation, legal process — long before they contact anyone. Portals dominate listing queries, but advisory queries are wide open. Agents and developers who publish genuine local market knowledge get cited in exactly the conversations that shape a buyer's shortlist.

Where the opportunity actually is

You will not out-rank the major property portals for "3BHK for sale in Jaipur." They have inventory scale, domain authority and structured listing data. That fight is lost and not worth funding.

But listing queries are a small slice of what buyers ask. The research phase is dominated by advisory questions the portals answer poorly or not at all:

  • "Is Mansarovar or Vaishali Nagar better for a family with school-age children?"
  • "What should I check before buying an under-construction flat in Jaipur?"
  • "How much have property prices moved in Jaipur over the last three years?"
  • "What does RERA registration actually protect me from?"
  • "Which builders in Jaipur have a good delivery record?"

These are the questions that form a shortlist. The source that answers them becomes the trusted voice before any agent is contacted.

Why agents are well placed to win them

You have something the portals structurally lack: specific, current, local knowledge. You know which micro-market moved, which builder slipped on delivery, which corridor gets congested. Portals publish generic city-level content because they operate in fifty cities. You operate in one.

An AI engine synthesizing an answer about a neighbourhood is looking for exactly that specificity — and it will cite the source that has it.

What to publish

Neighbourhood guides with real detail

One page per area you genuinely know. Price ranges by property type, who the area suits, commute realities, schools, infrastructure in progress, and honest drawbacks. The drawbacks matter — balanced content reads as credible to both readers and models, and one-sided promotion reads as marketing to be discounted.

Process explainers

Registration, stamp duty, home loan steps, RERA checks, documentation for resale. Buyers ask these constantly and most agency sites don't cover them. They're stable, evergreen, and highly citable.

Market data with dates

Quantified, dated, attributed observations about your market are the most citable content you can produce. "Average per-sq-ft rates in [area] moved from ₹X to ₹Y between 2024 and 2026, based on registered transactions" is precisely the shape a summarizer prefers — and refreshing it quarterly keeps the freshness signal alive.

Compliance and trust

Real estate is a high-stakes category where engines weight credibility heavily. Keep claims verifiable, state RERA numbers where applicable, date everything, and avoid guaranteed-return language — which is both a regulatory risk and a trust signal in the wrong direction.

The measurement angle

Build your prompt panel around research-phase questions rather than listing queries, since that's where you can realistically win. Track which neighbourhood guides get cited — that tells you which format works, and you replicate it across areas. See how to measure AI visibility.

Frequently asked

Can I compete with property portals in AI answers? +
Not on listing inventory — don't try. On advisory and local-knowledge questions you have a genuine structural advantage, because you have specific current knowledge of one market and they publish generic content across fifty.
Should individual agents do this, or only agencies? +
Individual agents may have the edge. A named person with a consistent profile, local expertise and reviews is an entity an engine can resolve and recommend. Personal brand and business entity reinforce each other.
How often should market data be updated? +
Quarterly at minimum. Stale price data is worse than none — it damages trust when a reader checks it, and freshness is weighted heavily in what gets cited.
